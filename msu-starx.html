The Program

About STARX

Engineering a robotic exoskeleton at MSU

MSU STARX is a student-led engineering team designing and building a wearable robotic exoskeleton — assistive technology with real-world applications for people with mobility impairments. The team combines mechanical engineering, biomedical research, and software development across a large interdisciplinary group of students.

Technology involved:

Robotic Exoskeleton Biomechanics Assistive Technology Embedded Systems CAD Design

The Challenge

The Problem

Engineering teams rarely communicate what they're building in a way that resonates with a general audience. STARX was doing groundbreaking work, but their social presence didn't reflect the significance — the content was inconsistent, overly technical, and wasn't growing the audience the program deserved.

The Opportunity

Deeply technical subjects become compelling when you tell the human story behind them. The opportunity was to position STARX not just as an engineering club, but as a team of students solving real problems for real people — making the technology feel tangible and the mission feel urgent.

My Approach

🎯

Lead with purpose, not specs

Every post anchored around who the technology helps — patients, caregivers, people with mobility challenges — before explaining how it works. Purpose creates emotional connection; specs create confusion.

👥

Humanize the team

Featured the individual students building the exoskeleton — their motivations, their backgrounds, their moments of breakthrough and frustration. People follow people, not projects.

🔬

Make complexity accessible

Developed a content framework for translating engineering concepts into plain language — using analogies, visuals, and progressive disclosure so content educated without alienating a non-technical audience.

📅

Build a rhythm

Established a consistent posting cadence with recurring content formats — team spotlights, build updates, milestone celebrations — so followers knew what to expect and had reasons to stay engaged.
