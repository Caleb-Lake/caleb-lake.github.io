The Origin

Built from nothing, for everyone

CSI Fridays was a student-produced weekly broadcast I co-created with a team of 5 Multi-Media students during my senior year of high school. The show premiered every Friday to the entire student body — highlighting school culture, student voices, and community moments in a polished video format. The goal was simple: make school feel like a place people were proud to belong to. That meant content that was funny when it needed to be, serious when it mattered, and always genuinely reflective of the people watching.

The Weekly Process

1

Monday — Ideation

Team brainstorm on content themes for the week. Surveyed student feedback from the previous episode to shape what we covered next.

2

Tue–Wed — Filming

Shot all footage across campus — interviews, event coverage, segments, and b-roll. I served as both on-camera host and behind-the-scenes producer depending on the scene.

3

Thursday — Edit & Polish

Full editing pass in Adobe Premiere — cuts, color, audio mix, graphics, titles. Turnaround had to be tight; there was no slipping the Friday air date.

4

Friday — Premiere

Broadcast to 400+ students during school. Collected live reactions and informal feedback to inform the following week's episode.

What It Built

Creative Skills

This was my real-world film school — writing scripts, directing segments, hosting on camera, and editing full episodes under pressure taught me more about storytelling than any class could. I learned to think visually and narratively at the same time.

Professional Instincts

Meeting a non-negotiable deadline every single week for an entire school year built discipline and composure I carry into every project. When things went wrong — and they did — you figured it out and delivered anyway.

Legacy

The show outlived my graduation

After I graduated, the school continued running CSI Fridays — which I consider the best outcome possible. Building something that sustains itself without you is the real measure of whether you built it right. The systems, the culture, and the creative standard we established were strong enough that the next team could carry it forward.
